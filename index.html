The real epiphany: it's not the 401k, it's the brokerage rate
Every young surgeon already knows to max the 401k/403(b) and backdoor Roth every year — that part is basically automatic and capped by law. Hospital-employed surgeons usually also have a 457(b), another $24,500 pre-tax that many never use. Fewer know about cash balance / defined benefit plans, which some private groups offer and which can shelter a large additional amount pre-tax depending on age and group structure — worth asking your group's financial advisor or CPA about explicitly, since it isn't always volunteered. But the single biggest lever after that, the one actually within your control every single year, is your taxable brokerage savings rate. Tax-advantaged accounts are capped. The taxable brokerage does not. Pay yourself first, monthly, on autopilot — treat the taxable brokerage contribution like a bill that's due before lifestyle spending happens, and raise that automated amount every time pay increases or a big fixed cost ends. When daycare ends, when loans are paid off — redirect that exact amount the same month, before it gets absorbed.

The worst path
Luxury car lease day 1. House year 1. Kids in private school immediately. Loan minimums only. Invest whatever is left — usually nothing. Each felt like a reward. Together they cost 10–15 years of mandatory call.
The path nobody warns you about
No loans, no luxury cars — and still saving under 10%. Three kids, full-time childcare for two (~$70k/yr), a North Jersey mortgage and property tax, and the ordinary cost of running a five-person household can absorb a $700–800k income. Nothing extravagant; it just adds up. The fix is not monthly discipline — it is deciding house price, school, and childcare with the real numbers in hand before they lock in, and redirecting childcare money to the brokerage the month it ends.

What changed in 2026
SAVE — eliminated. Struck down by federal courts. Borrowers moved to IBR or interest-only forbearance. No reinstatement expected.
RAP — new July 1, 2026. Repayment Assistance Plan. 1% of total AGI at $10–20k, rising 1 point per $10k bracket to 10% above $100k; minus $50/month per dependent; $10 minimum. No payment cap. Qualifies for PSLF.
IBR — still available. 10% of discretionary income (AGI minus 150% of the poverty line for your family size), never more than the 10-year standard payment. Usually lower than RAP for high-earning attendings. Qualifies for PSLF.
PSLF — intact. 120 qualifying payments at a nonprofit / government employer → 100% tax-free forgiveness. Best deal in medicine if your employer qualifies.
Non-PSLF IDR forgiveness — now taxable. Carrying loans 20–25 years on IBR/RAP without PSLF now triggers a taxable forgiveness event. For a surgeon, that could mean a $200–600k+ tax bill in retirement. The old "low payment, wait it out" strategy is now a trap.
Rates 2026–27: 8.07% for graduate unsubsidized loans, 9.07% for Grad PLUS (fixed at disbursement). Grad PLUS is closed to new borrowers after July 1, 2026; new borrowing is capped. Slider defaults to 8.1%.
